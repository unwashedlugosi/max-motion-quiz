Spelling doesn't count. Meaning does.

The 7 definitions (your teacher's exact words)

MotionChange in position
AccelerationThe rate of change in velocity/speed
Reference PointThe location of the observer of motion
VelocityDescribing a moving object using speed and direction
Constant SpeedA speed that does not vary or change
Negative AccelerationWhen an object's speed decreases (also called deceleration)
Positive AccelerationWhen an object's speed increases

Why velocity needs a direction

  • Speed alone only tells how far. You need which way to know where it goes.
  • Direction (north, left, toward the wall) is not distance (50 m).
  • No direction given = speed (S). Has a direction = velocity (V).
    12 cm per sec → S.   3 m/sec North → V.

Same speed, new direction = new velocity

  • The speedometer never moved, but the ghost (same velocity) and the real ball split apart.
  • Bouncing back, turning a corner, going in a circle: the speed can stay the same but the direction changes, so the velocity changes.
  • Walking 6 ft/s around corners: speed was constant, velocity was not.
  • Ball bouncing off the wall: it did not have a constant velocity.

Careful: these trip people up

  • Rate of change of motion (position) = speed. acceleration
  • Rate of change in velocity/speed = acceleration.
  • Total distance ÷ total time = average speed.
    60 km in 1 h, then 60 km in 3 h: 120 ÷ 4 = 30 km/h, not 40.
  • Velocity is speed and direction. distance
  • Steady 60 mph in a straight line: acceleration is zero. Zero acceleration ≠ stopped.
  • Slowing from 35 to 0 mph: velocity did change, it did accelerate, negative = deceleration.

Speed math: always show the work

s = d ÷ t
d = s × t   t = d ÷ s
Cover the letter you want.
  • Write the formula, plug in, then answer with units:
    S = d ÷ t = 150 ÷ 6 = 25 ft/s
  • Decimal trick: 180 ÷ 4.5 → double both → 360 ÷ 9 = 40 km/h.
  • More distance, same time → faster. More time, same distance → slower.

Distance-time graphs

  • Slope = speed. Steeper line = faster.
  • A flat line at 40 m: not moving, and not at the start. It's stopped 40 m away.
